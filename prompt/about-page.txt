Introduction to my work and therapeutic philosophy
I'm a licensed clinical psychologist, working with individuals across different life stages who are navigating emotional challenges, life transitions, or internal struggles that may be difficult to articulate.
In my therapeutic work, I integrate cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), and psychodynamic approaches — tailoring the process to each person's needs. I see therapy as a collaborative space that allows room for vulnerability, emotional connection, responsibility, and growth.

My areas of focus include trauma and post-trauma, anxiety, depression, grief and loss, self-esteem, relationships, parenting, and women’s health.

Professional Experience:

Over the years, I have accompanied individuals through diverse and complex life situations, combining in-depth academic knowledge with rich clinical experience. My professional path began with a Master's degree in Clinical Psychology, which I completed with honors, driven by a genuine commitment to my profession and to the people I work with.

I completed my four-year clinical internship at the Ramat Hen Mental Health Center. During the first year, I worked in a Day Hospital unit, where I treated patients struggling with depression, anxiety, and trauma. The daily encounters with individuals in deep distress provided me with significant clinical tools. In the following three years, I worked at the center's clinic, where I deepened my therapeutic work with diverse populations. In this capacity, I gained extensive experience in women's health, trauma, depression, anxiety, and more. I engaged in both individual and group therapies.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, a time when psychological distress became widespread, I provided short-term interventions that offered focused support to people in crisis. Later on, I participated in a therapeutic mission that provided immediate support to evacuees following the events of October 7th, and I treated medical teams in hospitals and health clinics through focused online sessions, which contributed to maintaining the mental resilience of those on the frontlines.

Today, I run a private clinic in Ramat Gan and also work online, with clients seeking help with a variety of difficulties and challenges. Alongside my therapeutic work, I am a guest writer for leading health columns in the field of mental health, with the aim of making important professional knowledge accessible to the wider public.
